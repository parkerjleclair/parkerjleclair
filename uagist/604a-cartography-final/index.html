GIST 604A – Cartography Final

Simplified Bedrock Geology of Adirondack Park, New York

Project Overview

This final project for GIST 604A Cartography presents a simplified bedrock geologic map of Adirondack Park in northern New York. The project was designed to preserve the major regional geologic patterns while reducing the complexity of detailed geologic mapping so the final map would remain clear and readable at a regional scale.

The final design combines generalized bedrock geology, water bodies, selected populated places, shaded relief, the Adirondack Park boundary, and a New York State locator map. The project applies cartographic principles including qualitative color, visual hierarchy, generalization, labeling, scale, typography, and inset map design.

Workflow

The map was created in ArcGIS Pro using geologic, hydrographic, elevation, and reference datasets. Because Adirondack Park crosses more than one New York State bedrock geology sheet, the source geology first had to be combined into a single working dataset before it could be generalized and symbolized.

  1. Clipped the Adirondack and Hudson–Mohawk bedrock geology datasets to the Adirondack Park boundary.
  2. Merged the clipped geology layers into one working bedrock dataset.
  3. Examined the original geologic unit codes using the New York State bedrock geology master legend.
  4. Created new fields representing broad geologic age, generalized rock type, and the final cartographic map class.
  5. Used field calculations to assign the original detailed units to broader age and lithologic categories.
  6. Used Pairwise Dissolve to remove internal boundaries between adjacent polygons assigned to the same generalized map class.
  7. Added water bodies and selected populated places to provide geographic reference without overwhelming the geology.
  8. Converted U.S. Geological Survey GNIS populated-place data from latitude and longitude coordinates using XY Table To Point.
  9. Mosaicked U.S. Geological Survey DEM tiles to create a continuous elevation surface for Adirondack Park.
  10. Generated a hillshade and used Extract by Mask to limit the terrain relief to the Adirondack Park boundary.
  11. Applied Unique Values symbology using a coordinated, muted qualitative color scheme.
  12. Created a New York State locator map showing the position of Adirondack Park within the state.
  13. Assembled the final 11 × 17 landscape layout with title, legend, place labels, scale bars, north arrow, locator map, projection information, and data credits.

Original Bedrock Geology Sheets

Adirondack Park spans portions of two New York State bedrock geology sheets: the Adirondack sheet and the Hudson–Mohawk sheet. These source maps contain a large number of detailed geologic units and extensive geologic linework.

At the regional scale used for this project, displaying every original unit individually would produce a very complex legend and make many of the mapped patterns difficult to distinguish. The original sheets below illustrate the level of detail in the source mapping before the geology was clipped, merged, grouped, and dissolved into broader cartographic classes.

Geologic Unit Generalization

The original New York State bedrock geology contains dozens of individual map units. Displaying every source unit separately would have produced a complicated legend and made many units difficult to distinguish at the final map scale.

Instead of preserving every original unit as a separate symbol, the geology was generalized using both geologic age and broad lithology. This preserved the dominant regional geologic patterns while reducing visual clutter.

Generalization Fields

  • AGE_GROUP – classified source units into broad geologic age groups.
  • ROCK_GROUP – grouped similar lithologies into broader rock categories.
  • MAP_CLASS – combined age and lithology into the final classes used for map symbology.

Final Geologic Classes

  • Cenozoic – Quaternary deposits
  • Mesozoic – Intrusive igneous rocks
  • Paleozoic – Carbonate sedimentary rocks
  • Paleozoic – Mixed sedimentary rocks
  • Paleozoic – Siliciclastic sedimentary rocks
  • Precambrian – Anorthosite and related mafic rocks
  • Precambrian – Granitic, syenitic and charnockitic gneiss
  • Precambrian – Mafic metamorphic rocks
  • Precambrian – Marble and calc-silicate rocks
  • Precambrian – Metasedimentary gneiss and quartzite

Additional distinction was retained among Precambrian rocks because they dominate much of Adirondack Park and are central to the region's geologic character. Younger units were generalized more broadly because they occupy smaller portions of the final map.

After classification, polygons assigned to the same final map class were dissolved. This removed unnecessary internal boundaries, including boundaries related to the original source map sheets, while retaining boundaries between the broader generalized units.

Original Geologic Unit Reference

The New York State bedrock geology master legend was used to interpret the abbreviated source-unit codes and determine the appropriate age and lithologic group for each unit.

Cartographic Design

The final color scheme uses muted colors rather than highly saturated categorical colors. Related units are organized by broad geologic age while differences in hue and value keep individual lithologies distinguishable.

Water bodies use a contrasting blue, while the Adirondack Park boundary is emphasized with a stronger blue outline. The Adirondack Park boundary is commonly known as the Blue Line.

A custom hillshade generated from USGS elevation data provides terrain texture beneath the geology. The shaded relief was kept visually subdued so that topography supports the geologic patterns rather than competing with them.

Only selected communities and reference features were retained. Limiting the number of labels and ancillary features helped establish a clear visual hierarchy in which the bedrock geology remains the primary subject of the map.
