GIST 604A – Cartography Final

Simplified Bedrock Geology of Adirondack Park, New York

Project Overview

This final project for GIST 604A Cartography presents a simplified bedrock geologic map of Adirondack Park in northern New York. The project was designed to preserve the major geologic patterns of the region while reducing the complexity of detailed geologic mapping so that the final map would remain clear and readable at a regional scale.

The final design combines generalized bedrock geology, major water bodies, selected populated places, shaded relief, the Adirondack Park boundary, and a New York State locator map. The project applies cartographic principles including qualitative color, visual hierarchy, generalization, labeling, scale, typography, and inset map design.

Workflow

The map was created in ArcGIS Pro using multiple geologic, hydrographic, elevation, and reference datasets. Because the Adirondack Park crosses more than one New York State bedrock geology sheet, the source geology first had to be combined into a single working dataset before it could be generalized and symbolized.

  1. Clipped the Adirondack and Hudson–Mohawk bedrock geology datasets to the Adirondack Park boundary.
  2. Merged the clipped bedrock geology layers into one working geologic dataset.
  3. Examined the original geologic unit codes using the New York State bedrock geology master legend.
  4. Created new fields for broad geologic age, generalized rock type, and the final cartographic map class.
  5. Used field calculations to assign the original detailed units to broader age and lithologic categories.
  6. Used Pairwise Dissolve to remove boundaries between adjacent polygons assigned to the same generalized map class.
  7. Added water bodies and selected populated places to provide geographic reference without overwhelming the geology.
  8. Converted U.S. Geological Survey GNIS populated-place data from latitude and longitude coordinates using XY Table To Point.
  9. Mosaicked U.S. Geological Survey DEM tiles to create a continuous elevation surface for Adirondack Park.
  10. Generated a hillshade and used Extract by Mask to limit the relief to the Adirondack Park boundary.
  11. Applied a color scheme using Unique Values symbology to distinguish geologic classes while maintaining a cohesive geologic-map appearance.
  12. Created a New York State locator map showing the location of Adirondack Park within the state.
  13. Assembled the final 11 × 17 landscape layout with title, legend, town labels, scale bars, north arrow, locator map, projection information, and data credits.

Geologic Unit Generalization

The original New York State bedrock geology contained dozens of individual map units. Displaying every source unit separately would have produced a complicated legend and made many units difficult to distinguish at the final map scale.

Instead of preserving every original unit as a separate symbol, the geology was generalized using both geologic age and broad lithology. This allowed the most important regional geologic patterns to remain visible while reducing visual clutter.

Generalization Fields

  • AGE_GROUP – classified units into broad geologic age groups.
  • ROCK_GROUP – grouped similar lithologies into broader rock categories.
  • MAP_CLASS – combined age and lithology into the final classes used for map symbology.

Final Geologic Classes

  • Cenozoic – Quaternary deposits
  • Mesozoic – Intrusive igneous rocks
  • Paleozoic – Carbonate sedimentary rocks
  • Paleozoic – Mixed sedimentary rocks
  • Paleozoic – Siliciclastic sedimentary rocks
  • Precambrian – Anorthosite and related mafic rocks
  • Precambrian – Granitic, syenitic and charnockitic gneiss
  • Precambrian – Mafic metamorphic rocks
  • Precambrian – Marble and calc-silicate rocks
  • Precambrian – Metasedimentary gneiss and quartzite

Additional distinction was retained among the Precambrian rocks because they dominate Adirondack Park. Younger units were generalized more broadly because they occupy smaller areas of the final map.

After the classification was completed, polygons assigned to the same final map class were dissolved. This removed unnecessary internal boundaries, including boundaries associated with the original source map sheets.

Original Geologic Unit Reference

The New York State bedrock geology master legend was used to interpret the abbreviated source-unit code attributes and determine the appropriate age and lithologic group for each unit.

Cartographic Design

The final color scheme uses muted colors rather than highly saturated categorical colors. Related units are grouped by geologic age while individual lithologies remain distinguishable. Water bodies use a contrasting blue and the Adirondack Park boundary is shown with a stronger blue outline to clearly define the mapped region. The Adirondack Park boundary is often referred to as "The Blue Line".

A custom hillshade generated from USGS elevation data provides subtle terrain texture beneath the bedrock geology. The hillshade was kept muted so that relief supports the geologic patterns rather than competing with them.
